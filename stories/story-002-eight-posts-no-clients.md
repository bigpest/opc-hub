# Eight Posts, Zero Clients

Second entry. The first one ended with five posts published and one save per copyable post, and I said the ending was unknown. This one ends with all eight published, three posts holding a save, and still no client.

## What I did that week

Nothing clever. I published the remaining three posts one day apart — the personal story as a pinned trust post, the list about substitutes for an internship, and the recap about being on the interviewer's side — and then I stopped.

Two things went wrong in the process and neither of them was in the copy.

**I published drafts containing placeholders.** Two of the eight drafts had a bracketed note where a real detail was missing — how many places I applied to, one specific interview scene. I published neither of those posts until I had deleted the note. What I did not do was invent the detail. An invented episode holds until it meets a reader who has been through it, and then it costs more than the post was worth.

**I filled the editor the wrong way and got two copies of the body.** This was a mechanical error, not a content one: the editor treats typed text as an insertion, not a replacement, so writing the body a second time appended it. It is worth naming because it produced a post whose visible text looked plausible — the duplication only showed as one merged paragraph, and the save-count would have hidden it.

## Three findings

**1. Saves track shape, and the shape list has two winners, not one.** Last entry said copyable posts get saved. That was half right. The template and the list both got saved at 13 and 17 views. But the story post got saved too, at 20 views — a post with no structure to copy and nothing to tick off. So the rule is not "copyable wins". It is: a post gets kept when the reader can either *take something out of it* or *recite themselves in it*. Diagnosis and counter-intuitive posts do neither — the reader nods, agrees, and leaves. Seventy-six views across four of them, zero saves.

**2. The offer post produced nothing, and the reason is structural.** Eleven views, zero saves, zero purchases. The post describes the service, names the price, and lists what it excludes — which is the correct shape for an offer. What it does not have is a way to pay. There is no store listing. So the most motivated reader arrives, reads a price, and discovers there is no buy button, which leaves a private message as the only path. Every offer post published without a payment path is an advertisement for a product that cannot be bought, and it burns the only audience you have.

**3. Fifty followers is a ceiling on reach, and eight posts did not move it.** Every post landed between 11 and 26 views. The best-performing post is the one written for the largest audience, not the one written best. Nothing in the writing changed the range. If reach matters, it is bought by the account's history, not by the copy.

## What I would do next, and what I actually did

Nothing, this week. Writing a ninth post is the easy move and the wrong one: the account has eight posts and three of them are demonstrably keepable, and none of those three has been reused, repinned, or turned into anything a reader can act on.

The concrete next step is converting the "nine years, seventeen times" post — which got 12 views and no saves despite being the sharpest criticism — into a checksheet a reader can run their own resume against, one line at a time. The argument is good; what it lacks is a form that can be held. That is the difference between a post that gets read and one that gets kept, and the data now says so twice.

## Still unresolved

No client. No payment path. The account tag is still forty-six posts of passive-income content plus eight of resume advice, and whether that mixture ever resolves into a topic is not something I can answer from here. Revenue: zero. Views: two hundred and something, across eight posts, in six days.

I am writing this down because the comparison is real and repeatable, and because a week where nothing sold is the part of these stories that usually gets left out.
