# Eight Cold-Start Posts for a Productised Skill

These are the scripts I wrote to sell a resume diagnosis from an account with thirty-three followers. The Chinese file contains the finished copy, ready to publish; this file explains the structure so the same shapes can be reused for any productised skill.

The finished Chinese text is the deliverable in this case — the posts only work in Chinese, on a Chinese platform. The structure below is the transferable part.

## The five shapes

Eight posts, five shapes. Each shape does a different job, and mixing them up is why most cold-start content produces nothing.

| Shape | Job | Reads like | Keeps? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diagnosis** | Make the reader recognise their own problem | "Here are the three ways this dies, and you have the second one" | No — it is consumed |
| **Counter-intuitive** | Overturn a default assumption | "The thing you were told to do is what is killing you" | No |
| **List with substitutes** | Give people who have nothing something to use | "You think you have no material; you have five kinds" | Yes |
| **Template** | Hand over a structure that can be pasted | "Three fields, filled like this" | Yes |
| **Personal story** | Build the reason to believe you | "I have this problem too, here is what it cost me" | Sometimes |
| **Offer** | Convert, with boundaries | "This is what it is, this is what it is not" | Sometimes |

The finding from running them: **the two shapes that get kept are the list and the template.** The diagnosis and counter-intuitive posts reached two to four times as many people and produced zero saves. Both keepable posts produced a save at six and twelve views. At that scale the only signal worth optimising is the save.

## The first three lines

On a platform where the post is collapsed after three lines, those three lines are the whole advertisement. They must do three things in order:

1. **A concrete scene** — a first-person action, with a number if there is one. "I was reading a resume and stopped at the third one."
2. **The failure named plainly** — not "you could improve it", but "the first screen is read in eight seconds and yours says nothing useful".
3. **A promise of what follows** — "three ways this dies, and you probably have the second one."

Do not open with credentials ("twenty years of experience"), with a question ("have you ever wondered..."), or with a definition. All three are scroll-past signals.

## Titles

One searchable noun plus one hook. Search terms are the nouns a person types into the search box when they already know they have a problem: "how to write a resume", "three years of experience", "no internship". The hook is the emotion or the number.

- Weak: "Some thoughts on resume writing" — no search term, no hook.
- Weak: "This one trick will change your life" — hook, no search term.
- Works: `How to write project experience｜one project, three lines`

Twenty characters is the hard limit on this platform; thirteen to eighteen is the useful range. The search term should also appear in the first line of the body and in the tags — all three places, or the search weight is split.

## Tags

Three layers, ten tags:

- **Broad** (two): the category everyone searches. `#resume` `#programmer`
- **Mid** (four to five): the sub-scenario. `#resume-editing` `#java-interview` `#job-hopping`
- **Narrow** (three to four): the exact phrase someone with this problem types. `#no-internship-resume` `#three-years-experience-resume`

Do not stack unrelated tags to chase reach. It moves the account's topic tag in the wrong direction, which is worse than no reach.

## Compliance boundaries

These are the lines that get a new account restricted, not just down-ranked:

- No contact details anywhere — not in the body, not in a comment, not in an image.
- No promises of outcomes: no "guaranteed interview", no "double your callbacks".
- No absolutes: no "best", "number one", "only".
- No invented numbers in a client-facing offer. Ranges and magnitudes only, and say that is what they are.
- Price in the body is allowed; the ability to pay must exist. A price with no purchase path turns every interested reader into a private-message negotiation, which is the violation you were avoiding.

## How the eight are ordered

Not by topic, by function:

1. **Diagnosis, the widest audience** — the biggest group gets the first post, because reach at cold start is scarce.
2. **Counter-intuitive, the second audience** — keeps the account from looking like a single-audience account.
3. **Diagnosis, the senior audience** — same shape, different level; senior readers decide whether the account is credible.
4. **Personal story** — the post that gets pinned. It is not for reach; it is for the reader who checks who you are before paying.
5. **Offer** — once per week maximum, always with dry content on either side. An offer post on a thin account reads as a storefront.
6. **List with substitutes** — the first post written for saves rather than reads.
7. **Template** — the second keepable post; by now the account has two posts that can be found by search.
8. **Story about being on the other side of the table** — the post that justifies the highest tier.

Two rules learned the hard way: **one post per day, maximum.** At this account size there is one audience pool, and two posts split it. And **do not rewrite copy because reach is low** — reach is account history; copy moves saves, not reach.

## What the Chinese file contains

The finished copy for all eight, including titles, tags and the offer post with its price and exclusions. Two posts are marked as incomplete on purpose: the personal story is missing one real episode, and the interview-recap post is missing one real interview scene. Writing those in would have meant inventing them, and an invented episode is the one thing that collapses on first contact with a reader who has been through it.
