# Resume Diagnosis in Sixty Minutes

A one-person service lives or dies on whether the delivery can be repeated without a team. This is the run sheet I use for a paid resume diagnosis: what happens before, during and after the hour, and the boundaries that keep it deliverable.

Use it as a checklist. Every step is meant to be performed, not discussed.

## Who this is for

- You already have paying clients or are about to take the first one, and the process has never been written down.
- You want a fixed scope that ends when the hour ends.
- You are selling judgement, not rewriting.

## What the client sends before the call

Ask for exactly three things. More than three and they will not send everything.

1. **The resume file**, in whatever format it currently exists. Do not ask them to clean it up first — the mess is the diagnosis.
2. **Two job descriptions**: one they want, one they think they want. The gap between them is usually the real problem.
3. **Three answers**, in one message:
   - How many applications in the last month, and how many replies.
   - Which stage you stop at: no reply, first call, technical round, final round.
   - The single bullet on your resume you feel least confident defending out loud.

If they cannot answer the third, that is already information: they have not read their own resume recently.

## Before the call — fifteen minutes of preparation

Work on the file before you speak to them. Three markings, no rewriting yet.

| Mark | Meaning | What you do with it |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Contains a problem, an action and an observable result | Keep. This is the model for everything else. |
| **B** | Real work, but written as a duty ("responsible for the order module") | Rewrite with the client's own words during the call |
| **C** | Unverifiable adjective ("proficient", "deep understanding", "rich experience") | Delete. Ask what it was meant to prove. |

Then count two things and write them down: how many bullets are **C**, and how many times the word "responsible" (or its equivalent) appears. Both numbers are usually larger than the client expects, and both are the fastest way to make the problem visible.

Finally, place the resume against the two job descriptions. Decide in advance which level they should be applying at. This decision is the part clients cannot make for themselves.

## During the call — sixty minutes, four segments

### 0–10 minutes: settle the target level

Ask one question first: *"If you got the job you are applying for, what would your first month actually be?"*

The answer tells you whether they are applying a level too high or too low. Most people who get no replies are applying one level above what their resume can defend. Say so in the first ten minutes. Everything after depends on it.

### 10–25 minutes: walk the resume, mark only

Read the resume with them, top to bottom, and say only three kinds of thing:

- "This one I kept — here is why it works." (the A bullets, named as models)
- "This one is a duty, not an achievement. What did it actually change?" (the B bullets)
- "What was this word meant to prove?" (the C bullets)

Do not soften. Do not rewrite yet. The point of this segment is that they start hearing their own resume the way a stranger does.

### 25–45 minutes: rewrite three bullets, out loud

Three, not thirty. Pick the three most load-bearing bullets and rebuild them in this order:

1. **What was wrong before you touched it.** Slow, error-prone, done by hand, nobody owned it.
2. **What you changed.** One concrete move: an index, a cache, a split of a conditional, a change of process.
3. **What changed after.** A number, or an order of magnitude if the number is genuinely lost.

The client speaks, you write. If they cannot produce the third element, do not invent it — write "roughly daily volume" or "single table of about a million rows" instead, and tell them why an honest magnitude beats a false precision.

Have them read the rewritten line out loud. If they stumble, it is not done.

### 45–55 minutes: decide where to send it

Three decisions, made explicitly:

- **Level**: which job titles this resume can now defend.
- **Channel**: where those roles are actually posted, and whether a referral is available.
- **Opening message**: two sentences, naming the single strongest evidence in the resume. Not a summary of the resume.

### 55–60 minutes: state the boundary

Say plainly what this price included and what it did not, and what the next step would cost if they want it. Do this even if they do not ask. It prevents the most common dispute: a client expecting a full rewrite because the hour felt generous.

## After the call — the deliverables

Send within one working day. Four items, no more:

1. The marked resume, with A/B/C annotations visible.
2. The three rewritten bullets, before and after, side by side.
3. A one-page application list: target titles, channels, opening message.
4. One paragraph on what would need to change for the next level up.

## The quality bar

A diagnosis is delivered when the client can do three things unaided:

- Point at any bullet and say whether it is A, B or C.
- Rewrite one more bullet themselves using the same three elements.
- State which level they should be applying at, and why.

If they cannot, the hour produced advice, not a deliverable.

## Pitfalls

- **Rewriting the whole resume inside the hour.** It feels generous and it destroys the price of the next tier. Three bullets is the scope.
- **Accepting a resume with no target job.** Without a target, "improve the resume" has no completion condition.
- **Inventing the missing number.** One fabricated metric turns the whole document into something the client cannot defend in an interview.
- **Charging by the hour without a fixed scope.** A solo operator has no bench; an open-ended hourly rate becomes a worse job than employment.
- **Leaving the boundary to the end and softening it.** Say what is excluded at the start and repeat it at the end.

## Time and money

- Preparation: 15 minutes.
- Call: 60 minutes.
- Write-up: 20–30 minutes.
- Total per client: under two hours, repeatable, no subcontracting.

---

This is a delivery run sheet, not a promise of outcomes. The client's interview results are not inside your control; what the hour can guarantee is that they leave able to read their own resume.
